Open most AI tools and you'll see a little menu of names to pick from. Those are [[model]] choices, and
the menu is not there to confuse you. It is the same as choosing a vehicle: bike, sedan, or moving
truck, each right for a different trip.

::figure id=choosing-the-vehicle label="Fig. 2 — The lineup" caption="Fast and cheap on one end, slow and powerful on the other."

## The trade is speed against power

The lighter models are the bike: fast, cheap, perfect for quick questions, rewrites, and everyday
back-and-forth. The heavier models are the truck: slower and more expensive per trip, but they carry
the hard loads, dense reasoning, long documents, tricky analysis, without breaking a sweat.

## A simple habit

Default to the fast one. Reach for the powerful one when a task is genuinely heavy and the quick model
starts to wobble. You don't need to memorize the names, which change often anyway. You need the
question: is this a quick errand or a heavy haul?

::check answer=0
You need a fast rewrite of a short email. Which vehicle?
- The bike, a light and fast model
- The moving truck, the most powerful model
- It makes no difference which you pick
::good Right. Light job, light vehicle. Save the truck for the heavy hauls.
::bad Not quite. A short email is a quick errand, so the light, fast model is the better fit.
::

"Models are vehicles. Match the vehicle to the trip."
