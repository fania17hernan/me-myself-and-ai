::analogy
A connector is a key you hand the AI to one of your rooms.
::

On its own, the AI is a sharp assistant locked in a bare office. It can only work with what you carry
in and read to it. A [[connector]] changes that: you hand it a key to one of your other rooms, your
email, your calendar, your files, so it can walk in, fetch what it needs, and file things back.

::figure id=set-of-keys label="Fig. 3 — The keys" caption="One key per room. You decide which ones leave the ring."

The part worth slowing down on is that keys are given one at a time, and knowingly. Connecting your
calendar does not hand over your email. And a key you gave can be taken back: unplug the connector and
that door locks again.

::note
Before you connect anything, ask the plain question: which room does this key open, and am I fine with that?
::

Used well, this is where the AI stops being a clever chat and starts doing errands: "check my
calendar and draft replies to the three emails about Thursday."

::say-it-back
"A connector is a key to one room. You hand it over on purpose, and you can take it back."
::
