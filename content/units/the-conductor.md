A single AI answering one prompt is a soloist. But real work often needs several steps in a row, or
several helpers at once: one to research, one to draft, one to check. [[orchestration]] is the
conductor who keeps them all in time, so the parts add up to one piece instead of noise.

::figure id=the-conductor label="Fig. 9 — In time" caption="Each part plays its bit; the conductor keeps the whole thing together."

## From one step to many

You've met the [[loop]], the same step repeated. Orchestration is the next level up: _different_ steps,
in the right order, each handing its result to the next. Research, then outline, then draft, then
fact-check. The conductor decides who plays when.

## Why it matters to you

You don't have to build this to benefit from it. When a tool "does the whole workflow" from one
request, there's usually orchestration behind the curtain. Knowing the word means the magic becomes a
method: a set of steps someone arranged, not a mystery.

::check answer=1
How is orchestration different from a loop?
- It's the same thing with a fancier name
- It coordinates different steps in order, not one step repeated
- It only works with a single AI helper
::good Right. A loop repeats one step; orchestration arranges many different ones.
::bad Not quite. A loop repeats a single step; orchestration coordinates different steps into one flow.
