::analogy
A database is a very tidy filing cabinet.
::

When an app "remembers" your customers, orders, or bookings, that information sits in a [[database]].
Picture a filing cabinet: labelled drawers, folders in rows, every record in a known spot. Nothing is
dumped in a pile, which is exactly why the app can find one order in an instant.

::figure id=filing-cabinet label="Fig. 5 — The cabinet" caption="Labelled drawers. Every record has its place."

The structure is the whole point. Because a customer folder always holds the same fields, name, email,
last order, the app can pull "everyone who bought in June" without reading every page. Messy piles
can't do that; labelled drawers can.

::note
When someone says data is "structured," this is what they mean: it lives in labelled drawers, not loose on a desk.
::

::say-it-back
"A database is a filing cabinet where every record has its labelled place."
::
